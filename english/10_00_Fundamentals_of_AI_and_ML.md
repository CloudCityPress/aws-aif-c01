# Domain 1. Fundamentals of AI and ML

Domain 1 is the vocabulary and lifecycle of AI and machine learning. Twenty percent of the exam comes from this domain, but its real weight is much larger because every other domain assumes the language and the mental models established here. If you cannot crisply distinguish AI from ML, supervised from unsupervised learning, or batch from real-time inferencing, the rest of the exam will feel ambiguous. Once those distinctions are sharp, the higher domains read as direct applications.

This domain matters strategically because it gives you the words that engineering and business stakeholders use when they negotiate AI projects. The difference between a project that ships and a project that stalls is often a small set of disagreements that come down to vocabulary: is "learning" the same as "training," is "inference" the same as "scoring," is a foundation model the same as a custom-trained model? Practitioners who can settle those questions in a meeting save weeks of cycle time.

After completing this domain you will be able to define the basic AI and ML terms used across the AWS portfolio, including newer terms such as agentic AI and large language model that are now central to business conversations. You will be able to recognize where AI/ML adds value and, just as importantly, where it does not. You will know the standard inferencing patterns and when each one is appropriate. You will also have a working mental model of the AI/ML lifecycle: how data flows from collection through training, deployment, and monitoring, and which AWS services participate at each stage.

The domain ties directly to your job because it gives you the language to evaluate AI proposals coming from vendors and internal teams. It lets you ask the questions that matter, such as which type of learning the model uses, what kind of data it expects, and how it will be served once it is in production. Those questions are the foundation of every AI project plan.

The chapters that follow break the domain into its three task statements. Task 1.1 covers basic AI and ML terminology, including the new V1.1 additions of generative AI and agentic AI, and the four standard inferencing modes (batch, real-time, asynchronous, serverless). Task 1.2 walks through where AI/ML provides business value and where it does not, including the new V1.1 question of when traditional ML is more appropriate than a foundation model. Task 1.3 covers the AI/ML development lifecycle end to end and maps each stage to the AWS services it most often runs on, including Amazon SageMaker AI, Amazon Bedrock, Amazon Q, Amazon Quick, and Kiro. By the end of this domain you will have the foundation that the rest of the book builds on.
