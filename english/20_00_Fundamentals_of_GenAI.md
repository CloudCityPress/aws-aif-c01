# Domain 2. Fundamentals of GenAI

Generative AI is now the largest single source of new AI projects in most enterprises. Domain 2 carries 24% of the exam weight and is the gateway to Domain 3, which goes into how foundation models are actually used. The chapters in this domain build the conceptual machinery you need to think clearly about generative systems: tokens, embeddings, foundation models, agentic patterns, and the cost-and-capability trade-offs that drive real procurement decisions.

The strategic importance of this material is that the cost structure and the failure modes of generative AI are different from those of classical machine learning. Generative systems are billed by tokens, not by predictions. They make hallucination errors that look authoritative. They produce results that change between runs even with identical inputs. Business stakeholders who understand these properties make better calls on which problems to attack with generative AI and which problems to leave to other tools.

After completing this domain you will be able to define the foundational generative-AI vocabulary, including the V1.1 additions of context engineering, token-based pricing, and agentic AI. You will be able to enumerate the typical generative use cases (summarization, AI assistants, translation, code generation, customer service, search, recommendations) and match them to the right model class. You will be able to weigh the advantages and the limitations of generative AI when scoping a business problem. And you will have a tour of the AWS portfolio for building generative applications, from Amazon Bedrock and Amazon SageMaker JumpStart to Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, Strands Agents, Kiro, and Amazon Quick.

The domain ties to your job in two specific ways. First, it gives you the language to participate in generative-AI design discussions without getting lost in jargon. Second, it gives you the mental model to challenge proposals that overpromise. When a vendor or an internal team pitches a generative-AI solution, you will know what questions to ask: what is the token budget per interaction, what fallback handles hallucinations, what guardrails catch toxic output, what part of the workflow is agentic versus single-shot, and which AWS service is being used as the underlying platform.

The chapters that follow break the domain into its three task statements. Task 2.1 covers the foundational concepts of generative AI, including the new agentic-AI material and the Model Context Protocol. Task 2.2 covers the capabilities and the limitations of generative AI, with explicit attention to hallucinations, nondeterminism, and the model-selection trade-offs that engineering teams care about. Task 2.3 covers the AWS infrastructure and services for building generative AI applications, with an emphasis on how the new V1.1 services fit alongside the established ones. By the end of this domain you will have the framework you need to engage productively in any generative-AI project on AWS.
