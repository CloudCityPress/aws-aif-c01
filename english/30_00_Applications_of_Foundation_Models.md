# Domain 3. Applications of Foundation Models

Domain 3 is the largest of the five at 28% of the exam, and it is the most application-oriented. Where Domain 2 builds the vocabulary of generative AI, Domain 3 covers what you actually do with foundation models in production: choose them, prompt them, customize them, and evaluate them. Most of the architecture decisions that distinguish a good foundation-model application from a bad one happen in this domain.

The strategic importance of this material is that foundation models are deceptively easy to demo and surprisingly hard to operationalize. A demo that looks brilliant in five queries can fail in twenty different ways at production scale. The four task statements in this domain are the four places those failures usually originate: design choices that did not match the business constraints, prompts that were not engineered for variability, customization choices that wasted money on the wrong technique, and evaluation regimes that did not measure what the business cared about.

After completing this domain you will know how to choose a foundation model based on cost, modality, latency, multilingual support, and the other criteria that show up in real RFPs. You will understand the inference parameters that change a model's behavior and how Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) and Amazon Bedrock Knowledge Bases solve the most common grounding problem. You will be able to choose between fine-tuning, in-context learning, RAG, and model distillation when a stakeholder asks "how do we make the model better at our domain?" You will be able to write effective prompts using the standard techniques (zero-shot, single-shot, few-shot, chain-of-thought) and recognize the risks of poorly designed prompts (exposure, poisoning, hijacking, jailbreaking). You will know the difference between training, fine-tuning, continuous pre-training, and distillation, and how to prepare data for each. And you will have a working set of evaluation methods, both technical (ROUGE, BLEU, BERTScore, LLM-as-a-judge) and business (task-completion rate, user satisfaction, cost per interaction).

The domain ties to your job because it is the level at which most business decisions about generative AI get made. The questions you will be asked in real projects are concentrated here: which model do we use, how do we make it good at our data, how do we keep it from saying something embarrassing, and how do we know if it is working? This domain gives you the framework to answer those questions in the language that engineering and product teams use.

The chapters that follow break the domain into its four task statements. Task 3.1 covers the design considerations and includes RAG and the AWS vector-storage options (Amazon OpenSearch Service, Amazon Aurora, Amazon Neptune, Amazon RDS for PostgreSQL). Task 3.2 covers prompt engineering, including the new V1.1 material on Amazon Bedrock Prompt Management. Task 3.3 covers training and fine-tuning, including data preparation and reinforcement learning from human feedback. Task 3.4 covers evaluation, including the new V1.1 emphasis on application-level metrics and on aligning evaluation with business objectives. By the end of this domain you will have a complete operating model for foundation-model applications.
