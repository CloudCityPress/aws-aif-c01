# Domain 5. Security, Compliance, and Governance for AI Solutions

Domain 5 is the operational and regulatory backstop for everything else in the book. It carries 14% of the exam, the same weight as Domain 4, but its practical importance to a deployed AI system is closer to the importance of brakes on a car. Without it, the rest of the system is dangerous regardless of how well it performs.

The strategic importance of this material has grown sharply with the move from experimentation to production AI. AI systems handle sensitive data, take autonomous actions through agents, and generate output that customers and partners receive directly. Every one of those properties widens the attack surface and the regulatory surface. Security teams now ask the same questions about AI workloads that they have always asked about the rest of the cloud estate, plus a new set of questions specific to generative and agentic systems.

After completing this domain you will be able to identify the AWS services and features that secure AI systems, including IAM roles and policies, encryption, Amazon Macie, AWS PrivateLink, and the new V1.1 additions of Amazon Bedrock AgentCore Identity, Policy in AgentCore, and Amazon Bedrock Guardrails. You will understand source citation and data-origin documentation, including data lineage, data cataloging, and Amazon SageMaker Model Cards. You will know the best practices for secure data engineering and the security and privacy considerations that are specific to AI: prompt injection, data leakage prevention, output filtering and validation, audit trails for AI interactions, and toxicity. And you will know how to use Retrieval Augmented Generation grounding, output validation, and confidence scoring to detect and reduce hallucinations.

The governance side of the domain covers the AWS services that support compliance: AWS Config, Amazon Inspector, AWS Audit Manager, AWS Artifact, AWS CloudTrail, and AWS Trusted Advisor. You will be able to describe the data-governance strategies that auditors expect (data lifecycles, logging, residency, monitoring, observation, retention) and the governance protocols that make those strategies work in practice (policies, review cadence, transparency standards, team training, and the AWS Generative AI Security Scoping Matrix).

The domain ties to your job because security, compliance, and governance are the topics that determine whether an AI proof-of-concept ever makes it to production. A model that the security team will not approve never sees a real customer. A system that the compliance team cannot audit never enters a regulated industry. The vocabulary in this domain is what you will use to align AI projects with the security and compliance functions in your organization, and to keep them aligned as the AI system evolves.

The chapters that follow break the domain into its two task statements. Task 5.1 covers methods to secure AI systems, including the new V1.1 additions for AgentCore Identity, Bedrock Guardrails, and the expanded list of AI-specific privacy considerations and hallucination-grounding techniques. Task 5.2 covers governance and compliance, including the AWS Generative AI Security Scoping Matrix and the standard data-governance strategies. By the end of this domain you will have the operational framework you need to keep an AI system safe, auditable, and compliant.
